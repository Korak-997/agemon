# Common AI Tells — November 2026

This list decays. Banning a look moves the model to the next default, so it never overrides a brand or a brief.

Use it as a calibration check on your own plan, not as a rulebook. When the brief or the project's existing design pins down a direction, follow that exactly, even if it asks for one of the looks below. These looks are only a problem when you reach for them because the brief left the axis open and they were the nearest default.

Dated 2026-11. Re-check it against fresh output before trusting it; the clusters below are what generated interfaces converged on at that date.

## The five clusters

1. **Cream, serif and clay.** A warm off-white page (around `#F4F1EA`), a high-contrast serif display face, and a terracotta or clay accent (around `#D97757`).
2. **Near-black with one acid accent.** A near-black page with a single bright green or vermilion accent doing all the work.
3. **Broadsheet.** Hairline rules, square corners and dense newspaper columns.
4. **The SaaS card kit.** Content cut into identical rounded cards, one radius on everything whatever its rank, the same soft grey shadow under each, and gradient washes used as decoration.
5. **Template chrome.** Parts that show up whatever the subject is:
   - a tracked-out all-caps label above every heading
   - middle-dot strings such as "A · B · C" and labels like "WORD — fragment"
   - tinted near-black standing in for black
   - a monospace face for small labels
   - an arrow appended to links and buttons
   - numbered markers on content that is not a sequence
   - one word in a headline set in italic, bold or colour
   - a hero built from a big number, a small label and a gradient accent

## How to use it

- Before building, name the palette, type and layout you plan to use. If any of them sits in a cluster above, ask whether the brief put it there or the default did.
- Derive the hero and the signature detail from the subject's own world: its materials, objects and vocabulary. A timer product opens on a running timer, not a metric card.
- A cluster that you keep because the brief asks for it is fine. Say that it came from the brief.
- When you revise away from a cluster, check the replacement is not the next-most-common default.
